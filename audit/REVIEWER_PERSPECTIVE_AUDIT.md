# Reviewer-Perspective Audit

Date: 2026-07-15

## Overall assessment

The repository is suitable for transparent exploratory work, but the current
study is not a validated observational dataset and does not support causal
claims. Computational reproducibility cannot resolve the measurement,
selection, and classification issues below. A submission should be framed as a
hypothesis-generating data and sensitivity exercise rather than confirmatory
evidence that network exclusion causes state collapse.

## Highest priority before submission

### 1. Measurement and sample construction

- **Severity:** Potential desk rejection or major revision
- **Expected improvement:** Very high
- **Feasibility:** Full resolution requires new coding; disclosure is feasible now

The 96 polity-period cases were not drawn from a documented sampling frame.
The first 54 records and the additional 42 records were generated by Devin in
two bulk edits without documented external-source retrieval. Eight principal
variables are AI-assisted subjective codings without a prespecified rubric,
outcome blinding, or independent raters.

**Recommended action:** Retain the current values only as an explicitly
AI-assisted exploratory dataset. For confirmatory use, define the target
population, prespecify inclusion criteria and coding rules, recruit independent
outcome-blinded coders, and report agreement and adjudication.

### 2. Post hoc reclassification and one-sided inference

- **Severity:** Potential desk rejection or major revision
- **Expected improvement:** Very high
- **Feasibility:** Disclosure is feasible now; independent confirmation requires new work

The technical-exclusion candidates were introduced after the initial analysis,
were not outcome-blinded, and all are coded as conquered under the main
assignment. The one-sided Fisher result changes from non-significant to
significant only after this reclassification. A p-value progression figure can
look like optimization toward a threshold.

**Recommended action:** Label the exercise as post hoc, emphasize effect sizes
and uncertainty rather than threshold crossing, report two-sided tests as a
robustness check, and treat the candidate set as a hypothesis to be tested with
an outcome-blinded rubric or an independent case sample. Consider moving the
p-value progression figure to the supplement.

### 3. Historically contested classifications

- **Severity:** Major revision
- **Expected improvement:** High
- **Feasibility:** Moderate

The source supplement transparently records contradicted or contested closure
classifications, including Western Rome, late Byzantium, late Ottoman,
Inca, Aztec, Ethiopia, Korean Empire, Switzerland, and Yugoslavia. It also
records contested outcome classifications. General historical overviews cannot
prove the absence of every closure or patron relationship.

**Recommended action:** Define each category with a decision tree, adjudicate
flagged cases using case-specific scholarship, and rerun all analyses with
contested cases excluded and under each plausible alternative coding.

## High priority

### 4. Statistical independence and model stability

- **Severity:** Major revision
- **Expected improvement:** High
- **Feasibility:** Moderate

Repeated periods and historically linked dynasties are treated as independent.
The multivariable models use a small non-probability sample, several correlated
subjective predictors, and sparse outcome cells. Wide or extreme confidence
intervals indicate unstable estimation.

**Recommended action:** Make cross-case dependence explicit, reduce the number
of predictors, report penalized logistic estimates where separation occurs,
and add grouped sensitivity analyses by polity lineage, region, and era.

### 5. Multiplicity and causal interpretation

- **Severity:** Major revision
- **Expected improvement:** High
- **Feasibility:** High for wording; moderate for reanalysis

The pipeline contains many Fisher tests, logistic models, mediation analyses,
bootstrap analyses, propensity-score analyses, and instrumental-variable
diagnostics. The AI-assisted coding, non-probability sample, and proposed
instruments do not identify causal effects, and multiplicity is not addressed.

**Recommended action:** Designate a small descriptive core, label remaining
analyses exploratory, report a complete analysis inventory, and avoid causal
terms such as effect, mechanism established, mediation, or identification
unless a defensible design is added.

### 6. Novelty and journal fit

- **Severity:** Major revision
- **Expected improvement:** Medium to high
- **Feasibility:** High

The proposed distinction between policy closure and structural network
exclusion is potentially interesting, but the scope ranges from antiquity to
modern semiconductors and artificial intelligence. This breadth can obscure
the historical contribution and makes comparability difficult.

**Recommended action:** Narrow the empirical claim, foreground the transparent
coding and sensitivity framework, and consider whether the manuscript is
better positioned as an exploratory shorter article, a methods/data article,
or a Surveys and Speculations contribution rather than a conventional causal
research article.

## Medium priority

### 7. Figures and tables

- **Severity:** Moderate revision
- **Expected improvement:** Medium
- **Feasibility:** High

Figures 1 and 2 both emphasize reclassification; Figure 2 emphasizes movement
across a p-value threshold. Figure 4 contains unstable multivariable estimates.

**Recommended action:** Lead with distributions and effect sizes with
confidence intervals, move threshold-focused graphics to the supplement, and
retain the regression forest plot only with clear instability warnings.

### 8. Reproducibility versus validity

- **Severity:** Moderate revision
- **Expected improvement:** High
- **Feasibility:** High

The revised repository can regenerate the analyses and documents from the
published CSV, but that demonstrates consistency with the published inputs,
not that the inputs are valid public-data measurements.

**Recommended action:** Keep this distinction in the abstract, methods, data
availability statement, README, and supplement. Archive a release with a DOI
before submission so the cited data and code remain fixed.

## Optional improvements

- Add an outcome-blinded recoding template and codebook for external reviewers.
- Publish machine-readable checksums for canonical inputs and generated
  statistical result files.
- Add a preregistered replication protocol using a new sample or held-out cases.
- Replace broad historical analogies with a smaller set of deeply documented
  comparative cases.

## Decision

Mechanical reproducibility can pass while scientific readiness remains
conditional. The present package should not be described as validated causal
evidence or as a public-data-derived historical measurement dataset.
